Definition

Per-participant median daily step count from participants' own Fitbit devices (Bring Your Own Device program), adults 18+ with at least 6 months of valid wear, monitored between 2018 and 2021 (median 4.0 years of monitoring).

The question asks for the top 10% of adults, and no p90 from a credible general-adult source could be retrieved. The only source in `sources` (All of Us Fitbit) stops at p75 (9,827 steps/day) and is a self-selected device-owner sample. Comparisons on other definitions, which must not be averaged together: (1) NHANES 2011–2014, wrist accelerometer with the OxWearables step algorithm, US adults 20+, N=8,762: mean 8,818 steps/day (95% CI 8,676–8,961); 48.6% of adults under 60 reach 9,000+ and 44.8% of adults 60+ reach 7,000+ (Shreves et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026, doi 10.1249/MSS.0000000000004009; paywalled, so any percentile table was not visible). (2) NHANES 2005–2006, hip ActiGraph, adults 20+: mean 9,676 uncensored or about 6,540 censored steps/day (Tudor-Locke et al. 2009, MSSE). The authors judged the uncensored figure implausibly high, and the two scales differ by about 3,000 steps. (3) HCHS/SOL, Actical hip accelerometer, US Hispanic/Latino adults 18–74, about 12,500 people, 2008–2011: median 6,770 steps/day. The paper publishes percentile norms but the full text was blocked. (4) Canadian Health Measures Survey 2007–2009, pedometer-scale accelerometer: mean about 9,500 for men and 8,400 for women; 35% reach 10,000+. (5) Japan National Health and Nutrition Survey 2022, pedometer, adults 20+: mean 6,465 for men and 5,820 for women (MHLW). (6) A Seoul community sample of 375 adults 65+ (ActiGraph GT3X+ hip, Pan 2025) gives p90 = 12,319 (men 65–74), 11,914 (women 65–74), 10,165 (men 75+) and 9,203 (women 75+). That is the wrong age range and a small non-national sample. Step counts depend heavily on the device and where it is worn (wrist or hip, pedometer or accelerometer, censoring rule), so values from different devices are not interchangeable. A follow-up pass should try to get the percentile tables from Shreves et al. 2026 or Moore et al. 2023, or compute weighted percentiles from the public NHANES 2011–2014 step files.
