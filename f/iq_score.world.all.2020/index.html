Definition

Minimum composite score on supervised intelligence tests that Intertel accepts as evidence of a score at or above the 99th percentile; Wechsler Full Scale IQ (WISC, WAIS, WPPSI), Stanford-Binet 5 IQ, Cattell scales. Stated criterion: 'The only criterion for membership is a score at or above the 99th percentile level on a qualifying and valid supervised test of intelligence.'

IQ is a norm-referenced score, so the percentile is built into the scale rather than measured in a population survey: Wechsler (WAIS-5, WAIS-IV, WISC-V) and Stanford-Binet 5 composites are scaled to mean 100, SD 15 against a US standardization sample (WAIS-IV n=2,200 adults aged 16 to 90, norms 2008; WAIS-5 n=2,020, norms 2024; WISC-V n=2,200 children, 2014), and 135 is the score those publishers' tables place at the 99th percentile; a Pearson sample report shows 133 already rounding to percentile rank 99 and 139 at 99.5. Percentile ranks are relative to same-age peers in that sample, not to the world population, and the mapping only holds on an SD-15 scale: older Stanford-Binet forms (SD 16) and Cattell scales (SD 24) put the 99th percentile at about 137 and 140 or 156 respectively. Scores on tests with old norms drift upward over time (Flynn effect), so high-IQ societies reject results from superseded editions. The 99th-percentile value comes from society admission thresholds that quote the publishers' norms, not from an independent empirical distribution; a full publisher percentile table is only in the paid technical manuals.
