Definition

Estimated median age at first marriage, men, United States, from the Current Population Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement. Census derives it indirectly from the share of each age group that has never married (Table MS-2 method), not from reported marriage dates.

The question asks about all adults, but neither the Census Bureau nor NCFMR publishes a combined median for both sexes. Both report men and women separately. This fact uses men. The matching women's medians are 28.4 (CPS ASEC 2025) and 28.8 (ACS 2024), so the median for both sexes together would be somewhere between the women's and men's values. A combined figure would need its own separate fact, since averaging the sexes is not a published statistic. Both sources estimate the median indirectly from never-married shares by age, not from a distribution of observed wedding ages. Higher percentiles (p90, p99) are not published, and the median applies only to people who marry. The two surveys differ in method and universe: the ACS uses ages 15 to 54. They usually differ by a few tenths of a year, and for men in these years they happen to match at 30.8. One secondary report (Peter Norman, US Marriage Age Report 2026) quotes 30.2 for men and 28.6 for women as the latest Census figures. Those values do not match the 2025 Census release, so the report is excluded.
