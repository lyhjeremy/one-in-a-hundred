To be in the top 1% of Canadian tax filers in Canada, you'd need a total income of at least $290,000.

Definition

Threshold of total income needed to be in the top 1%, 0.1% and 0.01% of individual Canadian tax filers. Total income covers earnings, investments, pensions, spousal support, other taxable income, government transfers and refundable tax credits. The unit is the individual tax filer, not the household or family. Thresholds use the national distribution.

The question asks about household income, but Statistics Canada publishes no top-1% threshold for households or economic families. The Canadian Income Survey decile tables (Table 11-10-0192-01) stop at decile upper limits. The 2021 Census household income brackets top out at an open-ended bracket, so they cannot locate the 99th percentile. The figure here is for individual tax filers, so it is lower than a true household top-1% cutoff would be for two-earner households. A Better Dwelling article reports 2019 thresholds of $250,300 (top 1%), $790,100 (top 0.1%) and $2,973,200 (top 0.01%) as household income. Those numbers are Statistics Canada's individual tax-filer thresholds for 2019, so the article mislabels them and is not an independent source. Secondary pages (MetaFilter, a Spring Financial blog) quote a 2021 threshold of $271,300 from the same StatCan series and are not independent either. Only one primary source measures this definition. Statistics Canada is the only body that holds the full tax-filer data, so no second independent source on the same definition was found.
