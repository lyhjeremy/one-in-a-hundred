Definition

The respondent's estimate of how much the property (house and lot, or condominium unit) would sell for if it were for sale, across all owner-occupied housing units in the United States. The value is self-reported, not an appraisal or a sale price.

No official, academic or platform source found publishes a 99th-percentile (or 90th-percentile) value of U.S. homes. The ACS gives the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles, plus a value distribution whose top bin, $2,000,000 or more, holds about 2.2% of owner-occupied homes. The top-1% threshold is therefore somewhere above $2 million, and no published figure pins it down. Getting an exact p99 would mean computing it from ACS PUMS microdata (VALP) or Survey of Consumer Finances microdata, which this task does not do. ACS values are owners' own estimates, not appraisals or sales prices, and they cover owner-occupied units only (rentals, vacant homes and second homes are excluded). Not usable as sources but worth noting: Zillow's top-tier ZHVI is the typical value of homes in the 65th-95th percentile range of each region, not a national p99. Redfin's luxury reports define luxury as the top 5% of estimated values within each metro. The Federal Reserve Distributional Financial Accounts report real estate held by net-worth percentile groups (for example, about $6.5 trillion held by the top 1% of households by wealth in Q1 2026), which describes household wealth, not a home-value distribution. The 2019-2023 ACS 5-year median was $303,400 (2023 dollars). It comes from the same survey, so it is not an independent second source.
