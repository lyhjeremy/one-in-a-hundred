Definition

Distribution of the current Handicap Index among male golfers holding a USGA Handicap Index (GHIN), grouped into 5-stroke bands. Plus handicaps are the band 'better than 0.0'.

Neither source publishes a top 1% value. Both put about 2% of US men with a Handicap Index below scratch (1.85% in about 2020, 2.0% at year-end 2025), so the top 1% of men are plus handicappers, better than 0.0. The exact plus value is not stated. Non-authoritative sites (CaddieHQ, Hireko) say 'about +2 or better', but they cite no fine-grained data, so they are left out. The USGA's own statistics page (usga.org handicapping-stats), which has finer bands, returned HTTP 403 and could not be read directly. Both listed sources rely on the same USGA GHIN dataset, a few years apart, so they are not independent. The population is only golfers who hold an official index: about 3.0 million US men in 2025, roughly 15% of US golfers. Casual golfers without an index are typically weaker, so this overstates skill relative to all golfers. For comparison on the same scale: US women (year-end 2025, n about 650,000) average 28.8, 0.9% hold a plus index, and the top 10% are about 14.9 or better. Globally, the R&A/USGA World Handicap System Data Report for 2025 (6.5 million golfers, 57 national associations) gives a male average of 16.9. One secondary summary says 0.9% of men worldwide are below 0.0. England Golf says about 1% of all English golfers are 0 or better. Plus indexes are written '+2.0' but are better than 0.0, so on a numeric low-is-better scale +2.0 corresponds to -2.0. The sex was not specified in the question; men were chosen because they are about 82% of US index holders.
