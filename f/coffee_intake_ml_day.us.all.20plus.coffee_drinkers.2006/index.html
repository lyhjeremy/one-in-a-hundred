Definition

Usual (long-run average) daily intake of coffee-containing beverages, caffeinated and decaf, among US adults aged 20 and over who drink coffee. NHANES 2003-2006, estimated from up to two nonconsecutive 24-hour recalls plus a food frequency questionnaire using the NCI usual-intake method, survey-weighted to the US adult population.

The question asks for the top 1% of coffee drinkers, but no credible source found publishes a 99th percentile of coffee volume. The highest published point is the 95th percentile of usual intake among US adult coffee drinkers: 1154 mL/day (39 fl oz, about 4.9 eight-ounce cups). The 99th percentile is higher than that, but it was not estimated here because the rules forbid extrapolating. Only one source on this definition was found. Comparisons that measure something else and were kept out of sources: (1) Rehm et al., Nutrients 2020 (NHANES 2011-2016, PMC7469044) reports a mean of 544.7 g/day among people who drank coffee on the recall day. That is single-day intake in grams, a different definition, and percentiles could not be retrieved because the page blocked access. (2) Caffeine surveys report caffeine, not coffee volume: the Dutch DNFCS 2012-2016 gives a P95 of 646 mg/day caffeine for the whole population, and Swiss menuCH 2014-2015 gives a P95 of 426 mg/day (coffee supplies 83% of it). (3) The National Coffee Association's cups-per-day figures are self-reported market research without published percentiles. The source covers the US only, adults aged 20 and over, and counts decaf as coffee. The data are from 2003-2006 and may understate current intake.
