Definition

Mean silent reading rate of healthy adult native speakers aged 17-60 reading normal text for comprehension, pooled across 190 published studies (1901-2019, mostly English, predominantly university students). Unweighted random-effects mean of study-level means. Words counted as published by each study (no character normalisation).

No credible source publishes a 99th percentile (or any percentile) of adult silent reading speed; the best sources give only means, between-study SDs, and descriptive ranges, so a top-1% value cannot be reported without deriving it. Measurement is not standardised: Brysbaert's pooled figure mixes short one-minute tests and hour-long texts, counts 'words' as each study did (English word ~4.6-5.1 letters), and covers native-speaking adults 17-60 who are mostly university students (WEIRD samples), not all adults worldwide. The only person-level SD (260 ± 87 wpm) comes from 48 self-selected highly educated book readers. Bookmate data are Russian-language and truncated above 300 wpm. Comprehension is only loosely controlled; speed without comprehension (skimming, RSVP) can reach 790-1652 wpm in lab tests (Rubin & Turano 1992, n=9) but is a different construct. Rayner et al. 2016 argue rates above roughly 500-750 wpm are not compatible with normal comprehension.
