Definition

VO2max measured directly by cardiopulmonary exercise testing (gas exchange) on a maximal treadmill test. Sample is US adults aged 20-79 without cardiovascular disease, from 8 US laboratories. Percentiles are given by sex and decade of age.

Neither source publishes a 99th percentile. FRIEND 2015 stops at p95 (59.8 mL/kg/min for US men 30-39), and HUNT 3 gives only mean 49.1 and SD 7.5 for Norwegian men 30-39. Any p99 figure would be an extrapolation the site has to make. The two samples also differ: the HUNT 3 mean (49.1) sits well above the FRIEND median (42.4), so the cohorts are not interchangeable and a plain average will blur that difference. Neither is a random national sample. FRIEND is US lab-tested adults without cardiovascular disease, and HUNT 3 is healthy Norwegian volunteers, so treating them as 'world' is a stretch. The 2022 FRIEND update (Kaminsky et al., Mayo Clin Proc 97(2):285-293, n=16,278 treadmill tests from 34 US labs) reports treadmill standards 1.5-4.6 mL/kg/min lower than the 2015 values. Its men 30-39 table could not be retrieved, and secondary blogs quoting it disagree (medians of 38.6 and 39.7 both appear), so it is left out of sources. Excluded on definition: (1) Cooper Institute/Cooper Clinic tables (reprinted by Garmin, p95 'Superior' = 54.0 for men 30-39) use VO2max predicted from treadmill time, not measured gas exchange. (2) Peterman et al. 2021 JAHA FRIEND tables cover patients with cardiovascular disease (men 30-39 treadmill p90 = 40.3), not the general population. (3) Smartwatch VO2 max estimates (Garmin, Apple) are algorithmic estimates on a different basis. Elite endurance athletes routinely exceed 70-80 mL/kg/min, so the true population p99 is probably above the FRIEND p95, but no credible source states it.
