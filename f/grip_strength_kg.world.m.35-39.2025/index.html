Definition

Absolute handgrip strength in kg, harmonised to a reference protocol: both hands tested, 3 repetitions per hand, summary statistic = maximum irrespective of hand, hydraulic dynamometer, seated, elbow flexed. Population-weighted GAMLSS norms built from Monte Carlo pseudo-data pooled from 100 studies (tests from 2000 onward). Values are for males aged 35-39.

Neither credible normative source publishes a 99th percentile for men's grip strength. The highest published percentile is P95 = 65.3 kg (Tomkinson 2025, men aged 35-39, the peak age band). Dodds 2014 stops at P90 = 64 kg but gives mean 51.6 kg and SD 10.1 kg at age 35. Any top-1% figure would have to be extrapolated, and I have not done that. The question gives no age, so the peak age band (35-39 in Tomkinson; 30-35 in Dodds) is used. Top-1% thresholds for all adult men would be lower because grip declines after about 40 (Tomkinson men 60-64: P95 59.3 kg). Protocol matters. Tomkinson harmonises to a seated hydraulic (Jamar-type) test and Dodds pools seated and standing tests, but both use the maximum single-hand reading. NHANES 2011-12 (Perna et al. 2016, J Strength Cond Res 30(3):867-874) is a different definition and is kept out of sources. It uses a standing Takei digital dynamometer, sums the best of each hand, and reports in pounds. US men 30-39 there: mean 216.4 lb, p20 184.7, p40 209.5, p50 216.1, p60 224.6, p80 245.3, with no higher percentiles. Dividing by roughly 2 to get one hand is only approximate.
