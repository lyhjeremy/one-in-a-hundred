The median among people in the United States who have retired in 2025 is 62.

Definition

Self-reported age at which current US retirees retired, in a nationally representative online survey of Americans aged 25 and older. Retirees report their own retirement age.

Both sources are surveys in which retirees report the age they retired, taken over the stock of current retirees. They are not cohort or administrative measures, and the two survey frames differ: EBRI's retirees come from an adult 25+ sample, Transamerica's are aged 50+. Other definitions were kept out of sources because they measure something else. (1) Gallup's April 2026 retiree survey gives a self-reported mean of about 61, not a median, and this figure was seen only through secondary sites. (2) The Center for Retirement Research at Boston College (from Census CPS data) defines the average retirement age as the age at which a cohort's labour force participation falls below 50%. For 2025 that is about 64.8 for men and 63.3 for women, published rounded as 65 and 63, with no combined figure. (3) The Federal Reserve SHED (2018 data, 2019 report) says nearly half of self-identified retirees retired before 62 and a quarter between 62 and 64. That fits a median near 62, but SHED does not publish a median. (4) Social Security claiming age is a different measure again. The question asks for the median person, which matches the survey medians. Answers to 'median retirement age' run about 62 for self-reported actual retirement and about 64 to 65 under the participation-based CRR definition.
