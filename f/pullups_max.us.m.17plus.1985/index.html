Definition

Maximum number of overhand dead-hang pull-ups in one untimed attempt. The sample is US public-school boys aged 17 and over (the oldest band, called '17+'), drawn as a national probability sample of students in grades 1 to 12.

Only one credible source matches this definition, and it does not cover adult men. It measures US schoolboys aged 17 and over in 1985, and it does not publish a 99th percentile. The closest published values are p95 = 17 and the sample maximum = 26 (n = 575). Mean 8.34 and SD 4.73 are also reported, but the distribution is skewed and has a floor at zero, so a normal approximation for p99 is unreliable. No values were computed here. No national statistics office tests pull-ups in adults (NHANES and the national Army test data cover push-ups, sit-ups and runs, not pull-ups), and no peer-reviewed adult general-population percentile table turned up. The following are comparisons only, not the same definition. (1) Male US Marines are a selected, trained population tested under PFT rules. A study of 19,678 active-duty enlisted USMC males reported a mean of 18.0 and SD of 5.0 pull-ups (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7745917, not fully verified because the page was blocked behind a browser check). Military Times reported that men aged 21 to 25 averaged 17.6 in 2017. A perfect PFT score needs 18 to 23 pull-ups depending on age. (2) Chinese university men are tested on pull-ups under the National Student Physical Health Standard, but no national percentile table was found and the protocol differs. (3) Fitness websites (ExRx, Marathon Handbook, FitnessVolt, Men's Health) repeat youth tables from the President's Challenge, which derive from this same 1985 survey, or use unsourced gym data. ExRx lists p95 = 15 for boys 17+, which does not match the survey's own table (17), so those sites were not used.
