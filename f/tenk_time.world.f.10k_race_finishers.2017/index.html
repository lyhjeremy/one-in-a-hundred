Definition

Official chip or gun finish times of female finishers in timed 10K road races, pooled across all ages, taken from race results RunRepeat collected. p1 is the time that only the fastest 1% of female finishers beat.

Only one source matches this definition (all female finishers of timed 10K races, every age). I could not find a second independent large-sample percentile table for that population. Sources not merged, because their definitions differ: (1) Runbundle (runbundle.com/stats/average-race-times/10k, updated Sep 2026) reports a women's mean of 1:08:44 and medians by age group (20-29 59:42, 30-39 1:00:14, 40-49 1:01:45, 50-59 1:04:54, 60-69 1:10:37, 70-79 1:19:04). Its data is 644,865 results that users entered into its calculators, not race results, and it gives no p1. (2) Running Level gives a women's average of 54:13 for 50 competitive UK races from 2016 to 2020. That field is faster and more selective, and the figure is a mean with no p1. (3) RunRepeat's 2019 State of Running (107.9M results, 1986-2018) gives women's average 10K times by age only. (4) A PeerJ study of the Bolder Boulder 10K (2008-2013, 191,693 performances) splits finishers into sex-specific sextiles, but I could not open the full text to read the cutoffs. Mismatch with the question: these are race finishers, a self-selected group that runs faster than women in general, and the pool is mostly US and UK races.
