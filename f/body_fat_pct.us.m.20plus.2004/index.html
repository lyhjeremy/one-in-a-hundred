Definition

Total (whole-body) percentage body fat measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (Hologic QDR 4500A) in examined NHANES participants. Values are survey-weighted and use multiple imputation for missing or invalid DXA scans. Row used: males, all races and ethnicities, age 20 years and older, crude.

The question asks for the top 1% leanest men, which is p1 on a lower-is-better scale. No credible population source publishes p1 for adult male body fat. The best national source, the NHANES 1999-2004 DXA tables, stops at p5 (17.4% for US men 20+; 15.5% for men 20-39), so the site will have to extrapolate below p5. I found only one source on this exact definition (Hologic DXA, US men 20+, population-weighted). Kelly, Wilson & Heymsfield 2009 (PLoS ONE, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0007038) publishes DXA %fat reference percentiles from the same NHANES 1999-2004 scans, broken out by ethnicity only. It is not independent of the CDC report, so it is left out of sources. Comparison, not for averaging: Imboden et al. 2017 (PLoS ONE, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0175110) measured Caucasian adults on GE Lunar Prodigy or iDXA scanners at two US university labs. This is a convenience sample, not population-weighted, and GE and Hologic scanners give systematically different %fat readings. Its table labels the leanest value as the '90th' percentile (fitness-ranking convention). Rewritten in conventional low-is-lean terms, its men's p10 is 11.0% at 20-29 (n=384, mean 21.1 ± SD 8.3, median 20.2), 11.2% at 30-39, 15.8% at 40-49, 20.0% at 50-59, 21.0% at 60-69 and 23.0% at 70-79. Its overall men's mean is 28.3 ± 9.4%. It has no all-ages row and no p1 or p5. Its leanest decile is well below NHANES p10 (17.4% at 20-39), so sample and scanner differences are large. Other methods (BIA, skinfolds, smart scales, calipers) are not comparable to DXA and were not used. The NHANES data are 20 years old, and US adiposity has risen since, so current percentiles are probably somewhat higher. Body fat rises with age, so an age-specific band (20-39) gives a leaner threshold than the all-adult row.
