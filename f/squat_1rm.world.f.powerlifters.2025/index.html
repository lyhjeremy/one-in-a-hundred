No source I found publishes a 99th-percentile squat for all women across bodyweights. The two sources above stop at p90 (162 kg, Strength Origins) or give only the median (108 kg, Stronger). The only p99 values come per weight class from Fitness Volt (fitnessvolt.com/strength-standards/squat/), which computes them from OpenPowerlifting raw competition results. Fitness Volt does not say whether lifters were drug-tested or what date range it used. Its refresh date is listed as 10 June 2026, but its metadata says 2 October 2026. Its female p99 values, in lb as published, are: 337 at 56 kg, 358 at 57 kg (n 25,812), 375 at 63 kg (n 36,890), 387 at 67.5 kg, 386 at 72 kg, 419 at 75 kg, 435 at 82.5 kg and 84 kg, 446 at 90 kg, 467 at 100 kg and 507 at 110 kg. Its p50 and p90 are 237 and 309 lb at 63 kg, and 254 and 337 lb at 75 kg. These are per class, so they are left out of sources. All three competition sources draw on the same OpenPowerlifting database, so none is independent of the others. They describe women who compete in raw powerlifting, a far stronger group than women in general. The question asks about women without qualification, and I found no measured squat data for the general population. For comparison, Strength Level (strengthlevel.com) uses self-reported estimated 1RMs from 1,024,968 female results logged from 2015 to 2026. It gives an average female squat of 76 kg and an 'Elite' (95th percentile of its users) standard of 129 kg at 60 kg bodyweight and 140 kg at 70 kg bodyweight. It publishes no p99. Symmetric Strength self-reported gym data, as shown on Fitness Volt, gives a women's p90 of only 206-265 lb across the 57-76 kg classes. Both are self-reported, so they are a different definition.
