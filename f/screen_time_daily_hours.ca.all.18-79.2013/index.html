Definition

Self-reported leisure screen time (TV, videos, video games, computer and internet use for email, games etc.) in hours per day, averaged over a typical week in the past three months. Work and school computer use is excluded. Canadian adults aged 18 to 79.

Only one source on this definition was found. The question asks about 'adults' with no country and does not say whether all screen use counts. No official or peer-reviewed source found publishes a p90 for total adult screen time (work plus leisure, all devices). This answer uses the Canadian self-reported leisure measure for ages 18 to 79, collected in 2012-2013. The authors say the figures are outdated, and current values are probably higher. It leaves out work and school screen use and is self-reported, so it is far lower than total device time. For comparison only, on different definitions that are not averaged here: Winbush et al., PNAS 2025 (doi 10.1073/pnas.2427311122) logged smartphone use objectively in a large US adult sample. Secondary write-ups report a mean of about 338 min/day on the phone, a median of 295 min/day on non-social-media apps and a median of 42.7 min/day on social media, with a strongly right-skewed distribution. The full text could not be retrieved to check these or to find percentiles. The Health eHeart study (PMC5102460, n=653, objective smartphone logging) reports a median of 38.4 h per 30 days (IQR 21.4 to 61.3). CHMS 2018-2019 reports 60.8% of Canadian adults at 3 h/day or less of recreational screen time. Belgium's 2022-2023 food consumption survey reports 33% (weekdays) and 41% (weekend days) of adults aged 18 to 64 at 4 h/day or more. NHANES 2015-2016 reports 65% of US adults watching TV or videos 2 h/day or more. Commercial survey figures (for example WhistleOut, 6.9 h/day on phones) are self-reported, non-probability samples and are excluded.
