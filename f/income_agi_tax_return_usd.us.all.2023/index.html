Definition

Minimum adjusted gross income (AGI floor) needed for an individual federal income tax return to fall into each top percentile. All returns filed for tax year 2023, excluding returns filed by dependents. The unit is the tax return (single filer or married couple filing jointly), not the Census household.

The question asks about household income, but the only official top 1% cutoffs found are for federal tax returns (tax units). A Census household can contain several tax units, and a married couple filing separately counts as two returns. Non-filers are not counted. AGI includes realized capital gains and excludes most untaxed transfers and employer benefits. As a result, the tax-return cutoff is not the same number as a household cutoff. Only one independent source exists on this definition: both entries come from IRS SOI, for two different tax years. Comparisons on other definitions, kept out of sources: (1) Survey of Consumer Finances 2022 household income for the previous calendar year (2021), tabulated by Arthur Kennickell for the CUNY Stone Center (https://stonecenter.gc.cuny.edu/changes-in-household-wealth-and-income-1989-2022-an-analysis-by-arthur-b-kennickell/). Search excerpts of that table give p50 $70,259, p90 $248,610, p95 $390,209, p97 $546,941 and p98 $742,586. Its 99th percentile could not be retrieved, but it is above $742,586, so on a household basis the top 1% cutoff is clearly higher than the IRS per-return figure. (2) CBO, The Distribution of Household Income in 2021 (September 2024), puts the top 1% of households at an average income before transfers and taxes of about $3.1 million (as quoted by Tax Foundation). No CBO threshold could be retrieved because cbo.gov blocked the fetch. (3) The Census CPS ASEC household tables (HINC series) publish only up to the top 5%, not a 99th percentile. All dollar figures are nominal for their year.
