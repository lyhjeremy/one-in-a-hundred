Definition

Estimated number of English lemmas known by American native English speakers aged 20. Lemmas are uninflected words with proper nouns excluded, taken from a list of 61,800 entries. Knowledge was measured by yes/no word recognition with pseudoword correction in an online crowdsourced test.

The question asks for the top 1% of adults, but the only source on this definition publishes no value above the 95th percentile. Its p95 for 20-year-olds is about 52,000 lemmas and for 60-year-olds 56,400 lemmas, so the true p99 lies above those figures, capped by the 61,800-lemma test list. The sample is self-selected online test takers, restricted to American native speakers and split by age. It is not a representative sample of all adults, and non-native speakers are excluded. Comparison on a different scale, not averaged: myvocab.info (BRAVE project) counts word families with an adaptive IRT test on about 29,000 self-selected native speakers. It reports p25 14,400, p50 17,100, p75 19,900 and p90 22,700 word families, with no p95 or p99. Its word-family definition differs from Brysbaert's base words, and Brysbaert's word-family median is 11,100. Older rule-of-thumb estimates (Nation; Goulden, Nation & Read 1990) put educated native speakers near 20,000 word families. Those figures are also word families and are not comparable with lemmas.
