Definition

Conditional median value of education installment loans (student debt) held by US families with any education loan debt, family (household) level, 2022 SCF wave

The question asks about student loan debt in the US. The SCF measures it per family among families that hold education loans, not per individual borrower, and a family can contain more than one borrower. Among all families the median is $0, because only about 22% hold student debt. One comparison is the Federal Reserve SHED (Report on the Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2024, May 2025, https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-higher-education-and-student-loans.htm). It is individual-level and self-reported, counts debt for the respondent's own education only, and publishes the median only as a bracket: 'between $20,000 and $24,999'. It also reports that 28% of borrowers owed under $10,000 and most owed under $25,000. That puts it in line with the SCF, but it is a different unit of observation and is left out of sources. The Cleveland Fed Economic Commentary 2024-10 restates the SCF figures (about $25,000 median, $47,000 mean) and is not independent. The SCF is known to understate aggregate student debt relative to Fed G.19 and NY Fed credit-bureau totals, by roughly 25% in one secondary estimate. Neither official source publishes p90 or p99 values. The research step returned the values in thousands and averaged the 2019 and 2022 waves; the grid was corrected to the 2022 median in dollars at build review.
